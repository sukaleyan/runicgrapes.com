wine beyond the glass

Wine has been woven into human culture for thousands of years — not only as something to drink, but as something to depict, to celebrate, to shape with hands and tools.

From the ancient winemaking cultures of Anatolia and the Caucasus to the vineyards of today, wherever wine took root, it left traces. In pottery and textiles. In carvings and paintings. In festivals and rituals. In the hands of craftspeople who continue to give form to wine's presence in our lives.

This is a vast field. It spans continents and centuries. It lives in museums and village squares, in ancient vessels and living workshops.

Runic Grapes exists to witness this world — to record it, to share it, and to amplify the voices still adding to it.

who i am

Spotting the wine in Saint-Émilion

I am an art historian who fell in love with wine.

I spent years studying images — learning to look closely, to ask where things come from, what they meant, how meaning changes over time. When I discovered wine, I found a new landscape for that way of seeing. Runic Grapes is where I follow that fascination.

Apart from this, I tend a tiny vineyard in Württemberg and make things — always inspired by wine, with whatever I can find.

what i do

I am on a continuous hunt for wine's traces in visual and material culture.

I look for imagery — the gods, the symbols, the gestures that recur across time and place. I look for artists and craftspeople whose work carries wine's story forward. I look for objects — handmade, vintage, one-of-a-kind — rooted in real craft.

What I find, I share. On the blog, I write about what I discover. In the shop, I gather objects worth seeing and owning. Through artist collaborations, I bring the work of makers to those who might never have found them otherwise.

And with Spot the Wine, I am building an app to bring all of this together — a way to document wine's cultural traces and share them with others.

Wine's story is still being told.
